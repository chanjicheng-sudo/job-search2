# LinkedIn copy

Rule for this file: **scope numbers only, no outcome numbers.** Scope figures tell a
reader how big the job was and belong here. Performance figures belong on the CV. See
notes/writing-preferences.md.

LinkedIn does not render markdown, so the role highlights use a pasted bullet character.

## About — her final version

> I am a crypto growth marketer. I own paid acquisition across five channels and 26
> markets, plus the creative, app-store and reporting systems behind it. My experience
> covers retail, wealth and institutional products in a regulated environment, and my
> background is in data-science research.
>
> The piece of work I am most proud of is the in-house AI UGC pipeline I built. It now
> produces Blockchain.com's main paid social creative at an average cost of $5 per video,
> which means the team does not rely on external creators.

LinkedIn truncates About after roughly two lines before the "see more" link, so keep
"paid acquisition across five channels and 26 markets" in the first sentence wherever
it lands.

### Two things to check before publishing

**Confidentiality.** This publicly states that her employer's main paid social creative
is AI-generated, and discloses the unit cost. Both are internal operational facts. For
most companies that is harmless self-promotion; for a regulated crypto brand it is
something Brand or Comms would want a view on, and "our ads are AI" is a line a
journalist or competitor could pick up. She is also an intern, which makes it less clearly
hers to disclose. Not a reason to cut it — it is the best sentence — but worth a quick
word with her manager, which costs nothing now and is awkward after the fact.

A version that keeps the force and removes the disclosure:

> It now produces our main paid social creative at an average cost of $5 per video, which
> means the team does not rely on external creators.

Dropping the company name makes it a statement about her work rather than a disclosure
about theirs. Anyone reading the profile sees Blockchain.com directly underneath anyway.

**Defend the $5.** Someone will ask what is in it. If it is generation cost only, prompt
iteration, review and editing time sit outside it, and a sharp interviewer will push on
that. Have the fully-loaded figure in mind even if the profile shows the clean one.

### What was fixed from her draft

- **It changed person mid-paragraph.** The first sentences were CV register with no
  subject, then it switched to "I built". On a CV the implied subject is the convention;
  on LinkedIn the About section is read as her speaking, so the switch lands as a jolt.
- **"You can't tell it's AI" was the weakest sentence, not the strongest.** Unverifiable,
  a superlative, and it invites a reader to go judge the ads themselves. The $5 figure
  replaced it: a business fact someone else effectively decided, rather than a quality
  claim about her own work. It also drops the contraction she avoids everywhere else.
- **"Current proudest achievement" is an interview answer**, slightly staged in a
  profile. Just say the thing.

## Blockchain.com — role highlights

> • Own paid search across 26 markets, from campaign structure and bidding through to
> budget allocation. Roughly tripled daily installs while keeping cost per install flat.
>
> • Run structured campaign tests across markets to decide what to scale and what to cut.
>
> • Built an in-house AI UGC pipeline so the team can produce ad creative without
> depending on external creators.
>
> • Lead creative across 9 channels. Write the briefs, direct production, and supply
> assets to external acquisition partners.
>
> • Automated the daily Apple Search Ads reporting and worked with Data Engineering, AI
> Engineering and Security to publish it as an internal dashboard.
>
> • Own app store conversion across 20 storefronts and Custom Product Pages, and review
> regulated messaging across all retail products.

Six is on the long side for LinkedIn, where people stop reading after four. To shorten,
cut the last two — app store work and the reporting build are the least likely to be what
a recruiter is scanning for.

Note this says "UK campaigns" rather than "GB" where the figure appears elsewhere: GB
reads as a data field rather than a country to a human reader.
