# What "early growth hire" means

Plasma lists "you were an early growth hire at a global consumer fintech" as a
nice-to-have. It means being one of the first dedicated growth people at a company — not
joining a growth team, but being the start of one.

## What it looks like day to day

No playbook, no baselines, no creative library, and probably no attribution beyond the
basics. Deciding what to try, trying it, pulling your own numbers, writing your own copy.
No designer on tap and no analyst to hand you a dashboard. The job is discovery — finding
which channels and messages work at all — rather than optimising something already known
to work.

## What they screen for

Range and self-sufficiency over depth in any one thing. Evidence of having built rather
than inherited. Comfort making decisions on thin data, and a bias toward shipping.
Crucially, whether you will actually do the work yourself, because for a while nobody
else will.

## Why this is better news than a senior title at a scaled company

Two years of commercial experience is a stretch for a £100–150K band. "Early growth hire"
softens that considerably, because what is being bought is not seniority — it is range and
willingness to build. Someone who ran £5m of spend inside an established structure with a
creative agency, an analyst and an MMP already wired up is arguably *worse* suited to
this. The AI UGC pipeline, the reporting automation and the unglamorous setup work are not
side details for this kind of role. They are the main qualification.

It also means the "small team needs someone willing to do the work, not just plan it"
close is exactly the right instinct. Keep it.

## The honest downsides, worth asking about

You own the measurement problem rather than inherit it — which matters, because the
funded-account attribution gap becomes yours to build, and she is not hands-on with an
MMP. There is also nobody senior to learn paid social from.

Two questions worth asking them:

- Who owns attribution today, and what is already instrumented?
- What does the growth team look like in twelve months — am I building it, or will
  someone be hired above me?

The second matters more than it sounds. Early growth hires frequently either grow into
leading the function or get layered when a senior hire arrives, and which one happens is
usually decided before you join.
