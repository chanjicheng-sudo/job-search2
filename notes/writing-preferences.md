# Writing preferences and output rules

Locked preferences for anyone (or any assistant) producing CVs, LinkedIn copy, cover
letters or application answers for Jessie. These were established by trial and error, and
several of them exist because a draft was rejected for breaking them.

## Her writing voice

For application answers, cover letters, DMs — anything written as her speaking.

- Short flat declarative sentences.
- **No contractions.** "could not", "is not", "do not".
- Plain vocabulary.
- No em dashes. No semicolons.
- No triadic lists with a punchline. No rhetorical reversals.
- She rejected a draft as "too polished". Err on the side of plain.

**When she pastes her own draft, edit it in place.** Fix grammar, fix facts, restore
numbers she dropped. Do not smooth the rhythm and do not substitute a rewritten version
of your own. Keep her own phrases even when slightly informal — "dirty work", "2
weeks-ish" — because they are recognisably hers. Flag the risk of a phrase if there is
one, then leave the decision to her.

Exception: in a casual DM, contractions are fine and stripping them makes it stiff.

## CV rules

- Number- and action-led bullets throughout.
- **Her bullet format wins.** When she supplies bullets, use them verbatim, and write any
  new bullets in the same shape: verb, what, then the figures with the change in
  brackets, e.g. "increasing CTR from 0.4% to 1.15% (+188%), reducing CPI from $25 to $6
  (−76%)". **Every "from X to Y" gets its change in brackets**: (+188%), (−76%), (13x).
  Never stack bare "from X to Y" pairs without the change. Do not reword her bullets into a results-first or "Grew X by Y" template. She
  objected to this being reverted repeatedly. Flag a factual risk once, then leave it.
- `[ADD: metric — what is needed]` wherever a figure would strengthen a claim and we do
  not have one. Highlight placeholders so they cannot be submitted by accident.
- Tailored to the specific JD. Reorder and reframe real work; never invent it. If the JD
  demands something she has not done, say so rather than papering over it.
- **Order bullets by strength, not by the JD's section order.** Mapping to the JD decides
  what goes in. Strength decides what goes first. The 114-response survey was rejected as
  an opening line: it is activity with no outcome. Do not lead with it on any variant.
- Strong-but-irrelevant areas compress to one line rather than being deleted. Breadth
  still signals range.
- UK conventions. One page. ATS-plain: no tables, no text boxes, no columns, no
  header/footer content, real bullet-list numbering, standard fonts.
- Deliver the hiring-manager read-out **before** the CV: what the JD actually screens
  for, honest fit, and the gaps that would get probed.

### The profile paragraph

This took five attempts. The rules that came out of it:

- **Profile carries scope numbers. Bullets carry outcome numbers.** This is the fix for
  the overlap problem. A profile that reuses the bullets' outcome figures reads as a
  compressed restatement of the CV below it.
- It must signal **ownership, innovation and hands-on work**.
- **Never write a figure-free summary built on hedges.** "Track record of", "hands-on
  experience of" — she rejected exactly that as "even weaker". Do not fix overlap by
  deleting numbers.
- Open with the profession, not with a duty. "Growth marketer specialising in X" works;
  "Owns paid acquisition across..." reads as a job description.
- No half-told before/after stories. "having led that expansion from two" was rejected as
  meaningless — the noun was missing and the clause was doing a bullet's job.

### Integrity check before any document goes out

- Check every "built from scratch" or "from zero" claim against the baseline figures in
  `candidate-performance-note.md`. Paid was already running when she arrived.
- Check that a profile claim has a bullet behind it. An earlier draft claimed automated
  reporting in the profile after the supporting bullet had been cut.
- Check scoped metrics are visibly scoped. GB CPI down 21.5% next to account CPI held
  flat reads as a contradiction unless the GB scope is stated.

### Cover letters

- **Never volunteer a gap.** "I have not built X before" was rejected. A cover letter
  argues the case. Gaps belong in the jd-read and OPEN-QUESTIONS, and are answered at
  interview if asked. Where a gap is real, show the nearest work she has done instead,
  without claiming the thing itself.
- This overrides the older plan in OPEN-QUESTIONS to state the AppsFlyer, Meta AEM and
  data-warehouse gaps in the Plasma letter.

## LinkedIn rules

**Scope numbers only. No outcome numbers.** Scope figures (26 markets, 9 channels, 20
storefronts, £50k monthly budget) tell a reader how big the job was and belong on
LinkedIn. Performance figures (3x installs, flat CPI, 21.5% CPI reduction, 5x
conversions) belong on the CV. A LinkedIn entry dense with outcome numbers was rejected as
"too detailed on the numbers".

Keep at most one clean multiplier if a bullet needs a payoff.

The About section is read as her speaking, so use first person and stay in it. Do not mix
CV register (no subject) with first person in the same paragraph.

## Delivery

- **Open every generated or changed CV's HTML for her to review**, rendered, not as a
  path or download card. Standing instruction from Oct 2026.

- Preferred delivery is a link she can open from any device, not a local file path.
- Google Docs has been blocked in the past: no Docs connector, and Drive's upload button
  opens a native OS file picker that browser automation cannot operate. The workaround is
  an HTML file with inline styles, opened in a browser, selected and pasted into a blank
  Doc — formatting survives the paste.
- Margins for a one-page CV in Google Docs: File → Page setup → Pages tab → 1.27 cm all
  round, then "Set as default" (applies to new documents only).
