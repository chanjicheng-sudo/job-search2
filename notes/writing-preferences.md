# Writing preferences and output rules

Locked preferences for anyone (or any assistant) producing CVs, LinkedIn copy, cover
letters or application answers for Jessie. These were established by trial and error, and
several of them exist because a draft was rejected for breaking them.

## Target roles (Nov 2026)

She wants to stay in **fintech and mobile apps**. Flag any role outside that before
building anything for it (for example, Lucky Egg, a physical party-games company, was
passed on for this reason).

## "Crypto" or "digital assets" (Nov 2026)

If a company's job ad does not use the word "crypto", or the company is not crypto-native,
write **"digital asset(s)"** instead of "crypto" in every document for it: CVs, cover
letters, form answers and messages. Rephrase so it still reads naturally ("new to digital
assets", not "digital asset natives"). Keep "crypto" only for crypto-native companies
that use the word themselves (for example Plasma, Kraken, Aave, BVNK, Noah).

## Naming the Earn product (Nov 2026)

Call Blockchain.com's "Earn" product **"yield product"** in every document ("the yield
product relaunch", "rebranded our yield product"). "Earn" means nothing to an outside
reader. The performance note keeps the internal name for reference.

## Her propositions and language — reuse these (Nov 2026)

She shaped these through many rounds of edits. Start from them next time instead of
writing new versions.

**"Why this company" paragraphs**
- Written from **her point of view**: the kind of work, challenge or culture that attracts
  her. Never a list of company facts (funding, milestones, user numbers), which she
  rejects as "facts dropping by an AI".
- The first sentence names the specific challenge or way of working in plain words, e.g.
  "you expect the same person to find the problem, do the analysis and solve it" or "you
  work across many regions, and that makes growth more challenging".
- Then one example from her own work, then what she would bring.

**Lines she wrote or approved**
- Hands-on roles: "a role where you have to get your hands dirty: writing the briefs,
  setting up the campaigns and tracking the results. I think you only really know why
  something is working, or why it is not, when you are involved in the whole process … it
  lets me make sure every piece of material is at the quality I want." Never call a role
  "generalist" in her letters.
- Strongest paid evidence for marketing letters: Apple Search Ads (2 to 26 countries, ~3x
  installs at flat CPI, 128 tests) plus ASO (20 storefronts, 20+ Custom Product Pages).
  Prefer this over the creative-briefs and partners paragraph, which she finds weak.
- "I use AI to solve marketing problems, whether improving current workflows or opening
  new acquisition funnels." (her preferred profile line, Nov 2026) / "My strength has
  always been problem solving."
- Multi-market: "Each country has its own compliance restrictions, and investors behave
  very differently from one market to the next. At Blockchain.com, we noticed that our
  users in Africa tend to take on more risk, while users in the UK are more balanced …
  I like work where one size does not fit all."
- Marketing to sales: "I like that marketing generates the leads for [BD / Investor
  Relations]. One-to-one outreach is hard to make efficient, while marketing can reach a
  wide audience and pass on the people who are ready."
- AI culture: "I noticed that you are hiring someone to bring AI into how your teams
  work, so I expect AI to be encouraged in everyday work. That suits me."
- Crypto in the real world: "you bridge crypto with the real world. I think DeFi is a
  cool concept, but if you cannot spend your money, it is not practical."
- Multinational: "one global network, but payment systems that have to be tailored to
  local regulations in every market."

**The AI UGC story, her version**
- Origin: ads relied on real creators, expensive, ran out fast, TikTok ads last about two
  weeks. She spotted competitors running AI-generated UGC ads and saw the downside of
  relying on creators, so she proposed building their own.
- Built in 2 weeks (never "evaluated 6 providers"). Always carry the full results when the
  bullet is used: production −75%, CTR 0.4% to 1.15% (+188%), CPI $25 to $6 (−76%), 600+
  installs in 7 days of the yield product relaunch. Never cut it to production time only.
- Lesson: "a project that cannot be replicated at scale is not a good project." The first
  version depended on her writing prompts and checking output, so she built a skill that
  generates the prompts and handed the workflow to other team members to scale. "No time
  or tokens wasted."

## Her writing voice

For application answers, cover letters, DMs — anything written as her speaking.

- Short flat declarative sentences.
- **No contractions.** "could not", "is not", "do not".
- Plain vocabulary.
- No em dashes. No semicolons. **No colons** in letters or answers: use commas instead
  (Nov 2026).
- No triadic lists with a punchline. No rhetorical reversals.
- She rejected a draft as "too polished". Err on the side of plain.

**When she pastes her own draft, edit it in place.** Fix grammar, fix facts, restore
numbers she dropped. Do not smooth the rhythm and do not substitute a rewritten version
of your own. Keep her own phrases even when slightly informal — "dirty work", "2
weeks-ish" — because they are recognisably hers. Flag the risk of a phrase if there is
one, then leave the decision to her.

Exception: in a casual DM, contractions are fine and stripping them makes it stiff.

## CV rules

- Number- and action-led bullets throughout.
- **Result-led, active verbs** (Oct 2026). Open with the result and a strong verb
  (Increased, Drove, Sourced, Eliminated, Grew), then "by" and the action. Keep the
  change in brackets after every "from X to Y".
- **No "with full accountability for efficiency"** or similar filler. She rejected it as
  meaningless. The figure (120% ROAS) carries the point.
- **AI UGC bullet (Nov 2026):** say she built it "in 2 weeks". Do not cite "evaluating 6
  AI model providers", which she does not see as proof of skill. Fold the yield product
  relaunch result (600+ installs in 7 days) into the same bullet rather than a separate
  one.
- **Always state the MRes as First Class Honours** on every CV (Nov 2026).
- **Only include when the job ad asks for it (Nov 2026):** the creative briefs bullet
  ("30+ creative briefs and 40 ads…") only when the ad mentions creative briefing or
  working with creative teams. The UK FinProm bullet only when the ad mentions compliance,
  regulation or financial promotions. Both are weak otherwise.
- **About 80% page fill is fine.** Do not pad a CV or cover letter to fill space.
- **ATS check on every new CV (Nov 2026):** before handing a CV over, go through the job
  ad line by line and make sure its exact phrases appear in the Skills lines or bullets
  wherever her record supports them (e.g. "project management", "stakeholder
  management", "email marketing", "B2C"). Never add a term she cannot back up.
- **Drop bullets with no result** unless they are the only evidence for a key requirement
  of that job (Nov 2026). The product pages / app-store bullet is the usual example.
- **No keyword counts** (47 to 2,597 keywords, 600+ keywords). Activity, not a result.
  She has rejected them. Use the outcome the work produced instead.
- **Her bullet format wins.** When she supplies bullets, use them verbatim, and write any
  new bullets in the same shape: verb, what, then the figures with the change in
  brackets, e.g. "increasing CTR from 0.4% to 1.15% (+188%), reducing CPI from $25 to $6
  (−76%)". **Every "from X to Y" gets its change in brackets**: (+188%), (−76%), (13x).
  Never stack bare "from X to Y" pairs without the change. Do not reword her bullets into a results-first or "Grew X by Y" template. She
  objected to this being reverted repeatedly. Flag a factual risk once, then leave it.
- `[ADD: metric — what is needed]` wherever a figure would strengthen a claim and we do
  not have one. Highlight placeholders so they cannot be submitted by accident.
- Tailored to the specific JD. Reorder and reframe real work; never invent it. If the JD
  demands something she has not done, say so rather than papering over it.
- **Always lead with the most impactful and relevant experience for that JD.** A theme she
  asks for (for example "problems solved with AI") goes into the wording, never into the
  order. On FlexTrade, AI bullets were put first and the lead-gen results, the strongest
  match, sat at 3 and 7. She rejected that.
- **Order bullets by strength, not by the JD's section order.** Mapping to the JD decides
  what goes in. Strength decides what goes first. The 114-response survey was rejected as
  an opening line: it is activity with no outcome. Do not lead with it on any variant.
- Strong-but-irrelevant areas compress to one line rather than being deleted. Breadth
  still signals range.
- UK conventions. One page. ATS-plain: no tables, no text boxes, no columns, no
  header/footer content, real bullet-list numbering, standard fonts.
- Deliver the hiring-manager read-out **before** the CV: what the JD actually screens
  for, honest fit, and the gaps that would get probed.

### The profile paragraph

This took five attempts. The rules that came out of it:

- **Profile carries scope numbers. Bullets carry outcome numbers.** This is the fix for
  the overlap problem. A profile that reuses the bullets' outcome figures reads as a
  compressed restatement of the CV below it.
- It must signal **ownership, innovation and hands-on work**.
- **Never write a figure-free summary built on hedges.** "Track record of", "hands-on
  experience of" — she rejected exactly that as "even weaker". Do not fix overlap by
  deleting numbers.
- Open with the profession, not with a duty. "Growth marketer specialising in X" works;
  "Owns paid acquisition across..." reads as a job description.
- No half-told before/after stories. "having led that expansion from two" was rejected as
  meaningless — the noun was missing and the clause was doing a bullet's job.

### Integrity check before any document goes out

- Check every "built from scratch" or "from zero" claim against the baseline figures in
  `candidate-performance-note.md`. Paid was already running when she arrived.
- Check that a profile claim has a bullet behind it. An earlier draft claimed automated
  reporting in the profile after the supporting bullet had been cut.
- Check scoped metrics are visibly scoped. GB CPI down 21.5% next to account CPI held
  flat reads as a contradiction unless the GB scope is stated.

### Cover letters

- **End each case paragraph with one line linking it back to the role** (Nov 2026).
  Numbers alone read as "numbers dropping"; say what the result means for the job.

- **Straightforward language only.** No compressed constructions like "MiCAR cost a major
  exchange its European clients". Say what happened in order: what changed, who it
  affected, what she did. She rejected the compressed version as impossible to follow.

- **The first sentence of a "why us" paragraph must name the specific thing.** Never open
  with "because of what this team does", "because of where your edge comes from" or any
  line that could be pasted into another company's letter. She has rejected this
  repeatedly. Research the company's edge, a recent launch or their own wording first,
  and put it in sentence one.

- **Never volunteer a gap.** "I have not built X before" was rejected. A cover letter
  argues the case. Gaps belong in the jd-read and OPEN-QUESTIONS, and are answered at
  interview if asked. Where a gap is real, show the nearest work she has done instead,
  without claiming the thing itself.
- This overrides the older plan in OPEN-QUESTIONS to state the AppsFlyer, Meta AEM and
  data-warehouse gaps in the Plasma letter.

## LinkedIn rules

**Headline exception (Nov 2026):** she wants numbers in the headline. Use short
multipliers and percentages (4x installs, 5x volume, −76% CPI, +12% retention), two or
three at most. The rest of LinkedIn follows the rule below.

**Scope numbers only. No outcome numbers.** Scope figures (26 markets, 9 channels, 20
storefronts, £50k monthly budget) tell a reader how big the job was and belong on
LinkedIn. Performance figures (3x installs, flat CPI, 21.5% CPI reduction, 5x
conversions) belong on the CV. A LinkedIn entry dense with outcome numbers was rejected as
"too detailed on the numbers".

Keep at most one clean multiplier if a bullet needs a payoff.

The About section is read as her speaking, so use first person and stay in it. Do not mix
CV register (no subject) with first person in the same paragraph.

## Delivery

- **Open every generated or changed CV's HTML for her to review**, rendered, not as a
  path or download card. Standing instruction from Oct 2026.

- Preferred delivery is a link she can open from any device, not a local file path.
- Google Docs has been blocked in the past: no Docs connector, and Drive's upload button
  opens a native OS file picker that browser automation cannot operate. The workaround is
  an HTML file with inline styles, opened in a browser, selected and pasted into a blank
  Doc — formatting survives the paste.
- Margins for a one-page CV in Google Docs: File → Page setup → Pages tab → 1.27 cm all
  round, then "Set as default" (applies to new documents only).
