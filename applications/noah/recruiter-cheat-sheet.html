Part 1 · The company

Noah in one line

"Noah lets businesses move money across borders through one API. Stablecoins underneath, local payout rails on top."
  • How money moves: fiat in, stablecoin in transit, fiat out. The customer never touches crypto.
  • Pays out on: bank, digital wallets, mobile money, cash. 150+ countries, 60+ currencies. Rules engine for automated payouts.
  • Founders: Shah Ramezani (co-founder, CEO). Thijn Lamers (co-founder, president, Adyen founding team, ex-EVP Global Sales).
  • Customers: remittances, fintech, marketplaces, payroll. 150+ new in 2026.
  • Partners: Circle, Paxos, NALA, Gnosis, Ledger, MiniPay, Portal.

The money goes to international transfer services, a New York office and US expansion, and hiring in engineering and compliance. Marketing is not mentioned.

Strategy

  • The bet: replace correspondent banking. Shah says the real problem is hidden FX cost, more than speed. Thijn wants stablecoin settlement to be invisible to the people using it.
  • Selling the shovels (Forbes, June 2026): cross-border FX payments only. No cards, no own stablecoin. Supplies rails to incumbents instead of competing with them.
  • Moving to enterprise: the October release is about major enterprises moving onto stablecoin. The company reports double-digit millions in ARR contracts.
  • Target: 4 to 5x revenue in 12 months.
  • Regions: emerging markets, where fees are highest. Fees to Sub-Saharan Africa average about 8%, many Asian corridors about 5%.

Competitors, in plain terms

They all do the same job: move money across borders for businesses. The difference is who owns them and what else they want from you. Think of them as delivery companies for money.

Noah's three differences: independence, reach, focus. Weakness: awareness. Bridge has Stripe's brand. BVNK will have Mastercard's.

"They are all B2B infrastructure. Noah does one thing, moving money across borders, and it is independent. Bridge is part of Stripe. BVNK is joining Mastercard. Ripple has its own coin. Noah can work with anyone. That is why Western Union picked it."
Avoid: saying competitors "focus on merchants". They are all B2B. Do not criticise them.

How Noah covers so many countries

None of them owns a bank in each country. They sign local payout partners. More partners, more countries. Noah's likely edge:

  1. Focus. Coverage is the whole product, so all the effort goes there.
  2. Emerging markets first. Fees are highest there, so the savings are biggest. Payout methods include bank, wallets, mobile money and cash. Mobile money is one method, not a headline.
  3. Partners with reach. NALA's platform (Rafiki) connects to banks and mobile money across several emerging markets. One deal adds many countries.
  4. Stablecoins make a new country cheap. Banks keep money parked in every country. Stablecoins move value on demand.

In the Western Union deal, Noah pre-funds, converts and pays out, and handles local compliance at each step.

"Coverage is Noah's main focus. It connects to local partners in each country, with a focus on emerging markets where fees are highest. Stablecoins make each new country cheaper to add. How do you decide which countries to add next?"
Remember: "widest coverage" is Noah's own claim. No independent comparison found.

Do not say

MiCA licence. Single source. If needed: "I read something about a MiCA licence. Is that right?"
BVNK as a place you applied. Say "BVNK is being acquired by Mastercard", not "is owned by".
Other coverage figures (160+, 200+, 100+, 70+). 200+ is Western Union's network. Use 150+ and 60+ only. If they quote another: "I have seen a few figures. Is 150+ the one you use?"
Market-size forecasts or competitor pricing. Sources conflict.
