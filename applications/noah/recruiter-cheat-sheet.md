# Noah: recruiter intro call cheat sheet

Digital Marketing, first dedicated marketing hire (B2B). Researched 9 November 2026.
Lines in quotes are said in your voice: no contractions, short sentences.
`[ADD: ...]` = a fact only you can fill in. Delete before the call.

## 1. Noah in one line

"Noah lets businesses move money across borders through one API. Stablecoins underneath,
local payout rails on top."

- **How money moves:** fiat in, stablecoin in transit, fiat out. The customer never
  touches crypto.
- **Pays out on:** bank, digital wallets, mobile money, cash. **150+ countries, 60+
  currencies.** Rules engine for automated payouts.
- **Founders:** Shah Ramezani (co-founder, CEO). Thijn Lamers (co-founder, president,
  Adyen founding team, ex-EVP Global Sales).
- **Customers:** remittances, fintech, marketplaces, payroll. 150+ new in 2026.
- **Partners:** Circle, Paxos, NALA, Gnosis, Ledger.

## 2. Recent news (October 2026)

- **7 Oct: seed closed at $38m** ($22m June 2025 led by LocalGlobe, plus $16m extension:
  Endeit, FJ Labs, LocalGlobe, Felix). Revenue **+538% year to date**, **31% month on
  month**. Money goes to international transfer services, a New York office and US
  expansion, and hiring in engineering and compliance. Marketing not mentioned.
- **21 Oct: Western Union picked Noah** to power the network behind **Receiver Redirect**.
  Recipients get a transfer as stablecoin (USDPT, issued by Anchorage Digital Bank), held in
  a Western Union wallet and spent with Stablecard. No cost or speed figures published.

"Two things stood out. You closed the seed at 38 million with revenue up 538% this year.
And Western Union picked Noah to power Receiver Redirect. The product is proven. The next
problem is awareness. That is this role."

## 3. Strategy

- **The bet:** replace correspondent banking. Shah says the real problem is hidden FX cost,
  more than speed. Thijn: make stablecoin settlement "invisible" to the people using it.
- **Selling the shovels** (Forbes, June 2026): cross-border FX payments only. No cards, no
  own stablecoin. Supplies rails to incumbents instead of competing with them.
- **Moving to enterprise:** the October release headline is about major enterprises moving
  onto stablecoin. Company reports "double-digit millions in ARR contracts".
- **Target:** 4 to 5x revenue in 12 months.
- **Thijn's Fortune op-ed (July 2026):** "Europe does not need more ambition. It needs to
  stop hiding it." European startups lose by underselling, not on product. This is your
  hook.

"I read Thijn's piece about European companies hiding their ambition. Noah has Western
Union, 538% growth and Adyen roots, but low awareness. That is the gap I want to close."

## 4. Competitors, in plain terms

They all do the same job: move money across borders for businesses. The difference is
**who owns them and what else they want from you.** Think of them as delivery companies
for money.

| | Owner | Plain version |
|---|---|---|
| Bridge | Stripe (about $1.1bn, Feb 2025) | Courier owned by a giant. Easy if you use Stripe. Awkward if Stripe is a rival |
| BVNK | Being acquired by Mastercard (up to $1.8bn, announced March 2026) | Courier joining a giant. Strong with large payment firms |
| Ripple | Independent, own coin (XRP) and stablecoin. Bought Rail | Courier that wants you to use its own vans |
| Conduit | Independent | Smaller courier, emerging-market corridors |
| **Noah** | **Independent** | **Only delivers. No owner, no coin to push. Claims the most places** |

**Noah's three differences:** independence, reach, focus.
**Noah's weakness:** awareness. Bridge has Stripe's brand. BVNK will have Mastercard's.

"They are all B2B infrastructure. Noah does one thing, moving money across borders, and it
is independent. Bridge is part of Stripe. BVNK is joining Mastercard. Ripple has its own
coin. Noah can work with anyone. That is why Western Union picked it."

Do not say competitors "focus on merchants". They are all B2B. Do not criticise them.

## 5. How Noah covers so many countries

None of them owns a bank in each country. They sign **local payout partners**. More
partners, more countries. Noah's likely edge:

1. **Focus.** Coverage is the whole product, so all the effort goes there.
2. **Beyond banks.** Mobile money and cash pickup open countries where many people have
   no bank account.
3. **Partners with reach.** NALA's platform (Rafiki) connects to banks and mobile money
   across several emerging markets. One deal adds many countries.
4. **Stablecoins make a new country cheap.** Banks keep money parked in every country.
   Stablecoins move value on demand.

In the Western Union deal, Noah pre-funds, converts and pays out, and handles local
compliance at each step.

"Coverage is Noah's main focus. It connects to local partners, including mobile money and
cash, not just banks. Stablecoins make each new country cheaper to add. How do you decide
which countries to add next?"

"Widest coverage" is Noah's own claim. No independent comparison found.

## 6. Do not say

- **MiCA licence.** Single source. If needed: "I read something about a MiCA licence. Is
  that right?"
- **BVNK as a place you applied.** Say "BVNK is being acquired by Mastercard", not "is
  owned by".
- **Other coverage figures** (160+, 200+, 100+). 200+ is Western Union's network. Use
  150+ and 60+ only. If they quote another: "I have seen a few figures. Is 150+ the one
  you use?"
- **Market-size forecasts or competitor pricing.** Sources conflict.

"I am a growth marketer in digital assets. I am at Blockchain.com now, across Retail,
Wealth and Institutional. Before that I was a Performance Marketer at Malaberg for just
over a year. I left a full-time job for Blockchain.com because I wanted to move into
financial services and digital assets. My strength is problem solving. I use AI to solve
marketing problems, whether improving workflows or opening new acquisition funnels. I
built an AI video pipeline that cut paid social cost per install from 25 dollars to 6. I
built a 100-operator prospect database that gave our Institutional team 20 active leads. I
want to build marketing from zero. Noah is at that stage."

## 8. Three stories

**A. AI UGC (owning a problem end to end)**
Creator ads were expensive and wore out in about two weeks. I proposed our own AI UGC and
built it in 2 weeks. Production time -75%. CTR 0.4% to 1.15%. Paid social CPI $25 to $6.
Yield rebrand: 600+ installs in 7 days. Built a prompt skill and handed it to the team.
"A project that cannot be replicated at scale is not a good project."

**B. Paid scaling (growth at a flat cost)**
Apple Search Ads from 2 to 26 countries, 9 to 121 campaigns. Daily installs about 4x
(200+ to 800+), CPI flat at about $1.50. 128 tests. Scaled 5 groups, cut 26. UK: CPI
-21.5%, installs +30.2%. US branded 178 to 373 a day.
If they quote the CV's 3x: **"The 3x was at an earlier point. It is about 4x now."**

**C. B2B pipeline (closest to Noah's buyers)**
100-operator prospect database, 20+ active leads for Institutional BD. During MiCA a major
exchange was losing European clients. I built an AI outreach list of 80 crypto publishers.
3 major high net worth clients.

**Backups:** perps survey of 114 users, retention +12%. Malaysia launch on my own
initiative, 4 HNW clients in month one. Malaberg landing pages, conversions 10 to 50+ a
day, 120% ROAS on £50k+ a month. Reporting automation, about 10 hours a month saved.
Iterable in 24 languages, open rate +10%.

## 11. First 90 days

- **Days 1 to 30:** dashboard first. HubSpot set up. Where do customers come from today.
  Talk to sales and 10 customers. Check what Google and AI tools say about Noah against
  Bridge and BVNK.
- **Days 31 to 60:** fix the website conversion path. Western Union and other customers as
  case studies. Founder posts on LinkedIn and X on a regular schedule. Test one paid or
  account-based channel on a target account list.
- **Days 61 to 90:** scale what works, cut what does not. Report pipeline, not reach.

## 12. Questions to ask (pick 3)

1. "What does the process look like from here, and who will I meet?" (always)
2. "Where do most customers come from today: founders' network, sales or inbound?"
3. "What made you decide to hire the first marketer now?"

Backups: "Who would I report to, and how does marketing work with sales?" / "What would
success look like after 6 months?" / "With the move to enterprise and the US, which
customer segment should marketing focus on first?"
