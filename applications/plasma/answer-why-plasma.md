# "Why are you excited about Plasma?"

Her three reasons, kept in her structure and her voice. Facts verified October 2026 —
see product-notes.md.

> From a product perspective I think Plasma's design makes sense. Take a payment product
> like Ready. Users had to move their stablecoins over Starknet. If your money is sitting
> on an exchange that does not support a nicher chain, you end up going from the exchange
> to a DeFi wallet, and then to the card wallet, before you can spend anything. That is
> three steps to move money you already own, and every step is a place to drop off.
> Plasma is built around stablecoins on one chain, and Binance already supports USDT
> withdrawals straight to Plasma, so that path collapses into one step.
>
> The second reason is timing. TradFi is trying to take a share of the crypto industry,
> and the users they bring in will not appreciate moving funds from wallet to wallet. I
> think they will pick one chain they are comfortable with and stay on it, and most of
> what they hold will be stablecoins and tokenised stock. A product built around
> stablecoins from the start is in a good position for that user.
>
> The third reason is that Plasma is new. There is no existing playbook, so the job is a
> lot of testing and iteration, and I would be setting the baseline instead of inheriting
> one. The crypto payment market is saturated with products but not with distribution. I
> could not find any active Plasma ads on the Meta Ads Library. So the hard part is the
> narrative, and that is the part I want to work on.

## What was changed from her draft, and why

- **Point 1 gained "every step is a place to drop off".** Her observation was a product
  one. That clause turns it into a growth one. Small addition, but it shows she reads a
  chain-hop as funnel leakage, which is the reason a marketer should care and the reason
  they should want her making the argument rather than an engineer.
- **Point 3 was reframed, because her version contradicted itself.** She said the market
  is saturated and that being new creates opportunity. As written those pull against each
  other, and "new means more growth opportunity" alone reads as "I like startups". The
  resolution is her own research: saturated with **products**, not with **distribution**.
  Nobody is advertising. Both halves then hold at once, and the weakest of her three
  points becomes the one that proves she did the work.
- **Point 2 was left almost exactly as she wrote it.** The reasoning is sound and "they
  will pick one chain and stay on it" is the sharpest observation in it. Only the
  preamble was cut: enthusiasm is better demonstrated by the argument than announced
  before it.
