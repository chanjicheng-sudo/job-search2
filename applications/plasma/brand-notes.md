# Plasma — brand tone and messaging

Read from plasma.org and plasma.org/docs, October 2026. They run two registers.

## Consumer voice (plasma.org — what paid social would support)

Deliberately mainstream fintech, not crypto. Verbatim:

- **"Make stablecoins your everyday money"** — the main headline
- **"Spend. Send. Earn."**
- **"Add local currency. Get dollars."**
- **"Built for money that moves globally."**
- "Send money across borders for free" · "Send instantly" · "Spend globally" ·
  "180+ countries"
- CTAs: "Get the app", "Download now", "Get the card"
- Running promotion: "Claim Core tier free for 6 months"
- Rewards lead visually: up to 4% cashback, up to 6% yield

Short imperative sentences, often three words. Plain vocabulary. Confident but not hyped:
no revolution language, no moon talk. Closer to Revolut or Wise than to a crypto project.

## Docs voice (developers and partners)

Same spine, more vocabulary: "a stablecoin-native financial network where people,
businesses, developers, and partners can hold, move, earn, and spend digital dollars",
"a global dollar account", "a purpose-built layer 1 for stablecoin payments". Selective
jargon — layer 1, EVM compatibility, gas abstraction — with good plain-language analogies:
"Banks were built around branches. Card networks were built around merchants."

## What they emphasise, in order

1. **Utility over asset.** "Everyday money" is the organising idea, present in both
   registers. Stablecoins as money you use, not something you hold.
2. **Dollars, specifically.** "Add local currency. Get dollars." "Digital dollars."
   "A global dollar account." The product's pull is access to USD, not interest in crypto.
3. **Free and instant movement across borders**, with 180+ countries as proof.
4. **Rewards** — cashback and yield.

What they pointedly do not say on the consumer surface: no Bitcoin anchoring, no
PlasmaBFT, no throughput figures, barely the word blockchain. All of that lives in the
docs. That separation is a deliberate brand decision and the most useful fact here.

## Three implications

- **Do not write "Send USDT with no fee".** Their own line is "Send money across borders
  for free". Naming the token is crypto-native framing; they say money and dollars on
  purpose. Writing in their register signals more than knowing their architecture does.
- **"Add local currency. Get dollars." identifies the customer.** That is dollarisation
  demand: people in soft-currency markets who want USD exposure and cheap remittance, not
  crypto enthusiasts. It explains the 180+ countries, and it plays to 26 markets of
  country-level creative plus Mandarin, Cantonese and Malay for Southeast Asian corridors.
- **"Spend. Send. Earn." is a ready-made test structure.** Three use cases, three pain
  points, which will not rank the same way in every market — remittance in one, dollar
  savings in another, cashback in a third. Rewards versus dollar-access is the obvious
  first hook test.
