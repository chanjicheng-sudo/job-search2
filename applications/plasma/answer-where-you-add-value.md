# "Where do you add the most value within Growth?"

## The strategy behind this answer

The trap in the question is that it invites "across the full funnel". At two years in,
breadth reads as not yet knowing what you are good at. Picking one thing narrowly signals
seniority.

The angle came from her own research, and it is better than the first version drafted for
her. The initial advice was to lead with decision velocity on spend. But if Plasma has no
paid social at all, there is no test loop to run — there is nothing to test. Creative
supply is the binding constraint first, and the loop makes it compound after. That
sequencing is more useful to them, and it is only visible because she checked the Meta
Ads Library.

One caveat to protect herself: the Ads Library shows currently active ads only, and a
company can run under a page name that does not match the brand. Say "I could not find
any active ads" rather than "you do not run paid social". If they do have something
running she looks thorough rather than wrong, and if they are deliberately pre-launch the
answer still lands.

## The answer

> The way I work in growth is to find the problem first, then propose a solution and
> build it. So before answering this question I went looking for the gap. Plasma has very
> little presence on paid social. I could not find any active ads on the Meta Ads Library.
>
> I think this is where I can add the most value. Paid search only reaches warm audiences
> who are already looking for a stablecoin product. Paid social is how you get to the
> mainstream users who are not searching yet. If you are starting from nothing on social,
> you will need enough creative to find out which message works.
>
> It was the same issue at Blockchain.com. They were heavily reliant on real creators, so
> once the assets were exhausted they ran out of creative fast. This was worst on TikTok,
> where an asset has a life span of about two weeks. I proposed using AI UGC as ads
> instead, and spent two weeks on the prompt engineering until you could not tell it was
> AI at first glance. It cut production from around 2 hours to 30 minutes per asset, and
> those ads now run on Google, LinkedIn, Meta and TikTok. That is the same pattern I
> would bring here.
>
> I also do a lot of the dirty work at Blockchain.com. Setting up ad accounts, conversion
> tracking, campaign structure and daily reporting. For a team the size of Plasma I think
> you need someone who is willing to do that work, not just plan it. I can set a channel
> up and run it on my own.

## Optional addition

The answer proves she can create creative supply. It does not yet prove she can turn
supply into efficiency. One sentence closes that, and it belongs immediately before the
dirty-work paragraph:

> I ran 128 tests across 26 markets and cut 26 campaigns. Moving around $21k of that
> spend reduced CPI on UK campaigns by 21.5% while installs rose 30.2%.

## Notes on wording

- **"dirty work" is kept deliberately.** It is her phrase and recognisably hers. The risk
  is that it can land as slightly resentful to a reader who does not know her. Naming the
  four tasks immediately after is what defuses it, so keep those together.
- **"SME" was swapped for "a team the size of Plasma".** Companies almost never describe
  themselves as an SME; it is a classification term and reads as diminishing when applied
  to a funded crypto startup.
- **Organic was left out.** For a paid role it splits the answer. See the note below.

## One refinement for an early-growth-hire framing

The advice to answer narrowly and leave organic out holds for the primary answer — she
still needs one sharp strength. But for an early growth hire, a flat refusal to range is
a negative signal, because the role will inevitably touch lifecycle, organic and
partnerships. So lead narrow, then show willingness: "paid social is where I would start,
and I would expect to pick up whatever else is unowned." Her organic social point from
the original draft has its home there.
