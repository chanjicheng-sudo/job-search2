# Kraken / Payward — QA & Operations, internal growth platform: hiring-manager read

## What the JD actually screens for

The title says QA, but read the bullets: approval queues, checking agent output against
standards, escalating risky output, multi-market and multi-language, compliance steps
built in. They are not hiring a software tester. They are hiring someone to **operate and
police a growth platform**, and the thing it produces is marketing output in a regulated
industry. The JD itself says the qualifying background can be "QA, testing, operations,
support, **or analytics**".

That changes the fit materially. A generic QA engineer would know regression testing but
would not know whether a campaign the agent built is sensible, or whether the copy clears
compliance. She knows both.

## Where she is strong, including two things buried on every other CV

- **Structured testing.** 128 structured tests across 26 markets, on top of an MRes in
  experimental design and causal inference. "A structured approach to testing. You can
  create and follow a test plan, and think about what could break" is her academic
  training plus real volume. Strongest single hook.
- **AI-native habits and prompt engineering curiosity.** They ask for someone who uses AI
  daily and is "curious about where they get things wrong". She evaluated 6 vendors and
  spent two weeks on prompt engineering specifically because the models kept producing
  wrong output. Most applicants will claim this line. She can evidence it.
- **Approval queues and checking output against standards.** Weekly Compliance reviews at
  Malaberg, regulated messaging review across all retail products at Blockchain.com. The
  most under-rated thing on her CV for this role, and it was a trailing clause in every
  previous version.
- **Comfort with data and spotting a wrong number.** Power BI, Clarity, funnel analysis,
  and the daily reporting she automated. Cutting 26 campaigns is evidence of acting on a
  bad number, not just noticing one.
- **Crypto plus multi-market.** Blockchain.com, 26 markets, 20 storefronts, four
  languages.

## Fit

A sideways move, not a step up, which is the easier kind to argue. She clears "operations
or analytics" on the stated bar and an unusual number of the nice-to-haves.

The pitch is not "I am a QA person". It is "I have operated the exact category of system
you are asking someone to test, in the exact regulatory context".

## Gaps that would get probed

No QA title and no bug-tracker evidence — Jira use unconfirmed. No regression testing. No
Mixpanel. And the real one: **why QA?** A growth CV landing on a QA req reads as an
any-crypto-job application until she says otherwise. That is cover-letter work, not CV
work.

## Three placeholders that decide the screen

These are not nice-to-haves, they are the difference between a shortlist and a pass:

1. **A specific error she caught.** One concrete instance of spotting something wrong in
   the data before it cost money is worth more than every scale number on the page,
   because it is the exact job.
2. **Did she reconcile the Streamlit dashboard against Apple Search Ads source data
   before launch?** If she checked the figures agreed, that is literal QA on a software
   product and should say so.
3. **Issue tracker and Mixpanel/Amplitude.** Both named nice-to-haves. Jira or Linear
   even lightly used is worth stating.
